    Case study

    NYC 311 Service Request Operations Dashboard

    An end-to-end reporting solution built on real New York City open data: almost a million service requests extracted with Python, cleaned and modelled in SQL Server, and turned into a three-page Power BI report for operations managers.

    995,652service requests
    Jul–Sep 2026period analysed
    3 pagesPower BI report
    100%row counts reconciled
    The problem

    311 handles thousands of non-emergency requests every day across dozens of city agencies. Managers need to see where demand comes from, which agencies miss resolution targets, and which requests have waited too long.

    The approach

    A paginated Python extractor, typed SQL Server tables, clean views that flag quality issues, a star schema with dimension views and a DAX date table, and a report designed around operational questions.

    The outcome

    A report that answers "how are we doing?" in seconds, with an agency SLA scorecard, backlog aging, key influencer analysis and an open data quality page.

      What the data revealed

      Each comparison shows what it's measured against.

      "4.56× more likely" compares two groups of resolved requests: those handled by one agency, and those handled by every other agency. It is the first group's rate of taking over 48 hours divided by the second group's rate. A factor of 1× would mean no difference.
      11.6% vs 69.9%

      Only 11.6% of Housing Preservation and Development's resolved requests closed within 48 hours, against 69.9% across all agencies. Its requests were 4.56× as likely to take over 48 hours as requests handled by other agencies.

      3.03× and 2.93×

      Housing & Building complaints were 3.03× as likely, and Sanitation complaints 2.93× as likely, to take over 48 hours as complaints in all other categories.

      2.16×

      Requests created between 7:00 and 18:00 were 2.16× as likely to take over 48 hours as requests created outside those hours.

      51.4%

      Just over half of all requests arrived online, ahead of phone (21.1%) and the mobile app (18.8%).
